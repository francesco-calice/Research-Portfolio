# Dark Matter Direct Detection

## Overview

This repository documents a Bachelor’s research project completed at Imperial College London under the supervision of Professor Stefan Söldner-Rembold.

The project critically examined the principles, experimental landscape and current limitations of direct searches for particle dark matter. We developed a first-principles Python model of spin-independent WIMP scattering and used it to study how detector properties and astrophysical assumptions determine exclusion limits across WIMP mass–cross-section parameter space.

## Project information

* **Institution:** Imperial College London
* **Project:** Bachelor’s Research Project
* **Supervisor:** Professor Stefan Söldner-Rembold
* **Period:** October 2025–January 2026
* **Research area:** Dark-matter phenomenology and direct detection
* **Project team:** Francesco Calice, Michelle Ng and Joseph Nolan

## Research undertaken

* Derived the spin-independent WIMP–nucleon scattering cross-section and associated nuclear-recoil rate.
* Implemented a numerical direct-detection model in Python using the Standard Halo Model and Helm nuclear form factor.
* Constructed a toy limit-setting framework based on Poisson statistics to estimate 90% confidence-level exclusion curves.
* Studied how detector exposure, energy thresholds, target nuclei and astrophysical assumptions affect experimental sensitivity.
* Compared the reach of leading experiments and detector technologies across WIMP mass–cross-section parameter space.
* Presented the results in a written report and internal viva.

The project received a mark of **86%**, the highest Bachelor’s research-project mark awarded in the year.
